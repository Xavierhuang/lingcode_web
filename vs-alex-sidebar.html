LingCode vs Alex Sidebar

The short version: Alex Sidebar puts AI next to Xcode. LingCode replaces Xcode. If Xcode is where you want to stay, Alex is the smaller, more natural change. If Xcode is the thing you are trying to spend less time in, that is the whole reason LingCode exists.

They are solving different problems

Alex Sidebar is a companion app that drives Xcode through macOS Accessibility — it reads what is on your screen and writes back into the editor. That is a genuinely clever design, and it means you keep Xcode exactly as it is: your build settings, your schemes, your Interface Builder, your muscle memory. You get chat about the selection, natural-language search over the project, image-to-code, and one-click fixes, without changing anything about how you build.

LingCode goes the other way. It is a native Mac IDE with the agent, the editor and the file tree in one window, and Claude Code, Codex and Gemini CLI built in. It does not sit beside Xcode; it is where you work instead. You still get real Swift, a real Xcode project and a real App Store build at the end — that part is not negotiable — but the day-to-day happens somewhere else.

Neither approach is a compromise of the other. They are answers to different questions.

Where each one is the better answer

Alex Sidebar is the better fit when…

  • You are an experienced iOS engineer and Xcode is not your bottleneck — the typing is.
  • Your project leans on Xcode-specific surfaces: Interface Builder, asset catalogs, complex scheme and build-setting work, Instruments.
  • You want the smallest possible change to an existing workflow, on an existing team, with existing habits.
  • You do not want to learn a second application.

LingCode is the better fit when…

  • You want the agent to build, read its own errors and iterate, rather than hand you code to paste. Build, run, diagnostics and LSP are tools it can call, not things you relay.
  • You want more than one agent session at a time — several Claude Code tabs, each an independent conversation, each surviving a relaunch.
  • You are not only writing Swift. Kotlin and Android, a backend, a website and deployment all live in the same window.
  • You want provider choice rather than a fixed roster — Claude, GPT, Gemini, DeepSeek and open-weight models behind one interface.
  • You are newer to iOS and Xcode itself is the obstacle. This is the case Alex explicitly is not built for, and it is the one LingCode was built for.

The honest trade-offs

What you give up by leaving Xcode. Interface Builder, Instruments, and the parts of the toolchain Apple only ships inside Xcode. LingCode does not reimplement those, and for some work you will still open Xcode. Anyone who tells you otherwise is selling something.

What you give up by staying in it. An assistant driving Xcode from outside is working through a narrow aperture. It sees the editor; it does not own the build. That ceiling is the same one we describe for Electron editors in why Cursor can't do what LingCode does — different mechanism, same shape of limit.

Price

Alex Sidebar is subscription-only, with paid tiers starting around $10/month and a short free trial. LingCode has a free tier that is genuinely usable, with Pro at $20/month — see pricing for exactly what each tier includes. If you want to try the approach before installing anything, /try runs in the browser.

Pricing on both sides changes; check each site before deciding on cost.

The fair recommendation

If you are shipping a mature iOS app with a team that knows Xcode well, install Alex and keep your workflow. If you are building something new, or you are one person doing iOS and Android and the backend, or Xcode is the reason you keep putting the project off — that is when replacing it starts to make sense.
